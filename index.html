Executive Summary

A Long-Term Institutional Partnership Model

SIRA — Scientific Research & Innovation Alliance proposes a long-term Institutional Innovation & Technology Transfer Partnership Model for educational institutions.

Not a conventional workshop

SIRA is not a workshop, seminar, or certificate-training provider. The purpose is to help a partner college develop the internal ability to: understand emerging technologies, identify real problems, develop student and faculty innovations, build prototypes, test and improve them, document the work, and explore technology-transfer opportunities.

Central Objective

Move the institution from:
Workshop Participation → Innovation Development → Institutional Capability

The partner college remains the primary innovation environment. SIRA provides technical exposure, engineering demonstrations, research guidance, mentoring, development support, and technology-transfer guidance.

SIRP Model

Student Innovation Resource Persons

A key component of the SIRA model is developing selected students as Student Innovation Resource Persons (SIRPs).

SIRP Responsibilities

  • Support junior students and innovation teams
  • Conduct approved internal knowledge-sharing sessions
  • Support innovation clubs/cells
  • Demonstrate technologies they genuinely understand
  • Assist faculty mentors and prototype development
  • Represent their institution in approved external activities
  • Present their institution's projects and innovation work

SIRP selection is not automatic for every student and involves institutional recommendation and SIRA evaluation.

Capability Multiplier

Instead of:

External Trainer → Students → Program Ends

The SIRA model develops:

SIRA → Students → SIRPs → Junior Students → New Teams → New Projects

This allows knowledge and capability to remain inside the institution.

Institutional Capability Multiplier

The long-term objective is for the partner college to develop its own: student innovation teams, SIRPs, faculty innovation mentors, innovation projects, prototypes, research documentation, technical demonstrations, innovation portfolio, internal knowledge-transfer system, and industry/community collaboration opportunities.

SIRA acts as a catalyst and technical partner, not a permanent substitute for institutional capability.

Conclusion

From Workshop Consumer to Institutional Capability

SIRA proposes a transition that transforms institutions from passive consumers to active innovators.

The Transition:
Workshop Consumer → Technology Learner → Innovation Developer → Prototype Creator → Student Innovation Resource Person → Institutional Innovation Capability

The partner college remains at the center of the model. SIRA provides the technical ecosystem, exposure, guidance and support required to help students and faculty convert knowledge into meaningful institutional innovation.

Long-Term Vision:
Partner institutions become self-strengthening innovation ecosystems capable of developing their own students, projects, prototypes and technologies, representing their own innovations externally, exploring technology transfer, and building opportunities for wider and national-level institutional recognition.
